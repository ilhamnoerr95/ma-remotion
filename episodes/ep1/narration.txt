# Part 1
Nobody in Edo expected the end of the world to fall quietly from the sky.
No invading army. No warning drums. Just a warm, black ash that tasted like burnt iron.
Within three days, the sun disappeared behind a ceiling of grey smoke.
Wells turned bitter. Rice fields withered into dust.
And the people who breathed in too much of that ash... stopped being human.
My name is Renjiro. I used to be a samurai sworn to protect a lord I couldn't save.
After that, I tied my sword shut with straw rope and promised myself one simple rule: keep your head down, trust nobody, and survive on your own.

# Part 2
That rule lasted until the seventh night. While scavenging for clean water near the edge of town, I smelled burning cedar.
The local herbalist's clinic was on fire. Commander Kagemori and his Iron Clan raiders were tearing the place apart.
They weren't looking for gold. They were hunting for Aoi-Kusa—a rare mountain herb that could filter the poison out of a person's lungs.
Inside the smoke, a young girl named Hana was cornered against a burning wall, holding a small wooden seed box like her life depended on it.
I told myself to walk away. It wasn't my fight. My sword was still tied shut.

# Part 3
Then Kagemori raised his iron club over her head—and my body moved before my mind could stop it.
I blocked his strike with my wooden scabbard, feeling the shock rattle straight up my arms.
Two guards lunged at my flanks. With one thumb, I snapped the straw rope off my hilt and drew my blade.
I didn't kill them—just the flat of the steel to their ribs, knocking the wind out of their lungs.
Before Kagemori could recover, I grabbed Hana's sleeve and pulled her through the back alley into the pouring ash. Just like that, my quiet life was over.

# Part 4
We hid inside an abandoned rice storehouse until midnight. Neither of us spoke a word.
Out in the dark, the Hollowed—people twisted by the black ash—were wandering the streets, listening for the slightest sound.
Inside, Hana was shivering, still clutching that wooden box to her chest.
I took off my coat, draped it over her shoulders, and broke my last cold rice ball in half.
She looked at me with wide, tired eyes and whispered, "If these seeds die, nobody survives the winter."
For the first time in months, I realized staying alive wasn't enough. We needed something worth surviving for.

# Part 5
At dawn, we made a run for the South Gate—only to find Kagemori already waiting with torches lit.
He smiled, thinking he had us cornered. "Hand over the girl, ronin," he called out, "and you can walk away."
His voice echoed across the empty square. That was his mistake.
From the rooftops above, dozens of Hollowed heard him shout—and dropped down like shadows.
Chaos erupted in seconds. While Kagemori's men fought off the infected in the dark, I threw a pouch of charcoal dust into their torches.
I sliced the wooden gate bar in half, and pushed Hana through the gap.

# Part 6
I slammed the heavy gate shut behind us and wedged my broken scabbard through the iron rings.
We had escaped Kurogane-juku, bruised and out of supplies, with nothing left except a handful of seeds and each other.
Ahead of us stretched the Silent Bamboo Forest—the only path leading toward the high mountain sanctuary.
Hana adjusted her cloth mask, gripped her wooden staff, and stepped beside me.
We didn't know what was waiting inside the mist.
All we knew was that if we stopped walking, the ash would bury us both.
